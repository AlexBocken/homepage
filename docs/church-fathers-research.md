# Church Fathers: research notes for the bio pages

Working notes gathered for the per-father pages under the Bible reader (life,
teaching, the Church's view today, particularities). Sources: Benedict XVI's
General Audiences on the Fathers (vatican.va, 2007–2010), the Catholic
Encyclopedia (newadvent.org), Wikipedia for the two non-patristic
commentators, the CCC and conciliar texts where cited. Everything below was
researched in English; the German goes into `src/lib/data/fathersBio.ts`.

Format per entry: dates and places · life · teaching · Church's view today ·
particularities · feast / title.

## Augustine of Hippo (354–430)
- Born Tagaste (Numidia) 13 Nov 354; mother Monica; rhetor in Carthage, Rome,
  Milan; nine years a Manichaean hearer; converted under Ambrose 386 (garden
  in Milan, "tolle lege", Rom 13,13f), baptised Easter 387; priest 391,
  bishop of Hippo 395/6; died 28 Aug 430 while the Vandals besieged Hippo.
- Works: Confessions, City of God, De Trinitate, De doctrina christiana,
  Enarrationes in Psalmos, Tractates on John, Retractations (reviews his own
  works at the end of his life).
- Controversies: Manichaeans (evil is privation of good), Donatists
  (sacraments valid regardless of the minister's holiness; unity of the
  Church), Pelagians and Semipelagians (grace precedes every good act;
  original sin; predestination). Opposed the death penalty for heretics.
- Church's view: "the greatest Father of the Latin Church" (Benedict XVI,
  9 Jan 2008); Doctor of the Church (1298, one of the original four Latin
  Doctors). Grace theology shaped Trent; his predestination hardened in
  Jansenism, which the Church condemned, and Trent read him with Orange 529
  (grace universal, freedom real). Faith and reason: "crede ut intelligas,
  intellige ut credas". "Our heart is restless until it rests in you."
- Particularity: In De Trinitate II–IV he holds that the Old Testament
  theophanies (the burning bush, Mamre, Sinai) were created effects produced
  through angels, in which any Person of the Trinity or the Trinity itself
  spoke, not appearances of the pre-incarnate Son. Every earlier Father
  (Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Hilary, Ambrose) read them as the Son.
  Augustine's reading became the Western norm.
- Feast 28 Aug. Title: Doctor gratiae.

## John Chrysostom (c. 349–407)
- Antioch; pupil of Libanius (rhetoric) and Diodore of Tarsus (exegesis);
  hermit 372–378 (ruined his health); deacon 381, priest 386; bishop of
  Constantinople 397/8.
- Antiochene, literal exegesis; more than 700 homilies survive (Genesis,
  Psalms, Matthew, John, Acts, Romans, Corinthians, Hebrews …), the largest
  corpus of any Greek Father. Preached against luxury, for the poor: "the
  poor are the altar of the Lord's body".
- Reforms at Constantinople made enemies (clergy, Theophilus of Alexandria,
  empress Eudoxia); deposed at the Synod of the Oak 403; exiled 404 to
  Cucusus, driven further; died at Comana 14 Sep 407, last words "Glory be to
  God for all things". Relics returned 438; rehabilitated.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church (1568); one of the three Holy
  Hierarchs; Pius X named him patron of preachers (1908). Benedict XVI
  (Sep 2007): his social teaching; the "school of the family" and of
  Scripture in the home. The Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom bears his
  name.
- Particularity: on Mt 16,18 reads the rock as Peter's confession of faith;
  on the Eucharist very realist ("the tongue is reddened with the Blood").
  Anti-Jewish homilies (Adversus Judaeos, 386/7) are read today as rhetoric
  of their time and rejected as a model.
- Feast 13 Sep.

## Jerome (c. 347–420)
- Stridon (Dalmatia); studied in Rome under Donatus; baptised c. 366;
  Trier, Aquileia; desert of Chalcis 374–379, learned Hebrew; ordained
  Antioch; Constantinople under Gregory Nazianzen 380; secretary to Pope
  Damasus 382–385, began revising the Latin Gospels; Bethlehem from 386 with
  Paula and Eustochium, monastery; died 30 Sep 420.
- Vulgate 384–405: Gospels revised from Greek, Old Testament translated from
  Hebrew (hebraica veritas), against Augustine's preference for the
  Septuagint. Trent 1546 declared the Vulgate "authentic". Commentaries on
  the prophets, Matthew, Galatians; De viris illustribus; letters.
- "Ignorance of Scripture is ignorance of Christ" (Comm. in Isaiam, prol.;
  DV 25).
- Controversies: Rufinus and Origenism (broke with his old friend), Jovinian
  (Mary's virginity, merit of virginity), Helvidius (perpetual virginity,
  "brothers of the Lord" as cousins), Vigilantius (relics, vigils), the
  Pelagians (Dialogue against the Pelagians 415), yet the CE notes a lean
  toward merit that reads semi-Pelagian.
- Particularity: doubted the deuterocanonical books (prefaces mark them as
  read for edification, not doctrine) while translating them anyway; the
  Church kept them (Trent). Famous temper; the lion of legend.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church (original four). Benedict XVI (Nov
  2007) on Jerome's love of Scripture; Francis' apostolic letter
  "Scripturae Sacrae affectus" 2020 for the 1600th anniversary.
- Feast 30 Sep.

## Ambrose of Milan (c. 340–397)
- Born Trier, son of a praetorian prefect; lawyer, governor of Aemilia-
  Liguria at Milan; acclaimed bishop 374 while still a catechumen, baptised
  and consecrated within a week (7 Dec).
- Learned Greek, read Philo, Origen, Basil; introduced allegorical exegesis
  and Eastern hymnody to the West (Ambrosian hymns, "Aeterne rerum
  conditor"); De officiis ministrorum, Hexaemeron, De mysteriis, De
  sacramentis, on virginity.
- Faced the Arian court (basilica conflict 385–6, sit-in with hymn
  singing), refused to hand over a church; had Theodosius do public penance
  for the massacre of Thessalonica 390: the emperor is in the Church, not
  above it. Converted and baptised Augustine 387. Augustine watched him read
  silently (Confessions VI,3).
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church (original four). Benedict XVI (Oct
  2007): catechesis of the mysteries, "lectio divina", the Church's freedom
  before power.
- Particularity: held the Angel of the Lord of the Old Testament to be
  Christ (the reading Augustine later dropped). Strongly Marian (Mary type
  of the Church). Rite of Milan bears his name.
- Feast 7 Dec.

## Gregory the Great (c. 540–604)
- Roman senatorial family; prefect of the city 572/3; monk 574, turned his
  house on the Caelian into St Andrew's monastery; deacon, apocrisiarius in
  Constantinople 579–586; pope 3 Sep 590 to 12 Mar 604, first monk pope.
- Works: Moralia in Job (35 books, monastic allegory), Regula pastoralis
  (the bishop's handbook for the whole Middle Ages), Dialogues (Life of
  Benedict), Homilies on the Gospels and Ezekiel, 850 letters.
- Fed Rome during plague and Lombard sieges, negotiated with the Lombards
  himself; sent Augustine of Canterbury to England 596; "servus servorum
  Dei"; called himself so against the patriarch's "ecumenical" title.
- Exegesis: threefold sense; the moral sense above all; "Scripture grows
  with its readers".
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church (original four). Benedict XVI (May/Jun
  2008). The attribution of Gregorian chant to him is legend (9th c.); some
  works (the Sacramentary) are later compilations.
- Feast 3 Sep (formerly 12 Mar).

## Bede the Venerable (672/3–735)
- Northumbria; given to Wearmouth at seven, Jarrow from 682; deacon 19,
  priest 30; never left the region; died 26 May 735 dictating a
  translation of John.
- Ecclesiastical History of the English People 731; commentaries on much
  of Scripture compiled from the Fathers (the source the Catena readers
  know); De temporum ratione: made anno Domini dating standard; hymns,
  Lives of the abbots.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church 1899 (the only English one); the
  title "Venerable" is his by ancient usage (Aachen 836).
- Particularity: exegesis almost wholly allegorical, mediating Augustine,
  Jerome, Gregory to the medieval world; careful about sources ("I have
  laboured to state the truth").
- Feast 25 May.

## Origen (c. 185–253/4)
- Alexandria; father Leonides martyred 202; head of the catechetical school
  at 18; self-mutilation (Eusebius; disputed); ordained in Caesarea 230,
  which Demetrius of Alexandria contested; moved to Caesarea 231; tortured
  under Decius 250, died of the effects at Tyre.
- Hexapla (six-column Old Testament); the first systematic theology (De
  principiis); commentaries and homilies on almost every book; Contra
  Celsum. Threefold sense of Scripture (body, soul, spirit) after 1 Thess
  5,23; the spiritual sense is the goal.
- Condemned positions (Origenism, not necessarily Origen's own teaching in
  its later form): pre-existence of souls, apokatastasis (universal
  restoration, even of the devil), a subordinationist tendency in the
  Trinity; controversies 400 (Theophilus, Jerome vs Rufinus), 543
  (Justinian's edict), 553 (Constantinople II, anathemas against
  "Origenists").
- Church's view: not a saint; the CE calls him no promoter of heresy;
  Benedict XVI (Apr/May 2007) calls him "one of the most remarkable" and
  praises his exegesis and the "primacy of Scripture"; DV and the CCC draw
  on his theory of the senses (CCC 115–118).
- Particularity: read Scripture as a body with a soul; allegory taken to
  extremes (the Gospels' contradictions intended); universalism the reason
  his name never got the "Saint".

## Cyril of Alexandria (c. 370/380–444)
- Nephew of Theophilus, present at the Synod of the Oak 403 against
  Chrysostom; patriarch 412; conflicts with Jews of Alexandria, the prefect
  Orestes (Hypatia's murder by a mob 415 is laid at his door by critics;
  no source makes him the instigator).
- Against Nestorius from 428: Mary is Theotokos because the one Person of
  the Word is the subject of both natures (hypostatic union); Twelve
  Anathemas; Council of Ephesus 431 (opened before John of Antioch's party
  arrived); Formula of Reunion 433 with the Antiochenes.
- Commentaries on John, Luke (homilies), the Pentateuch (Glaphyra), the
  minor prophets, Isaiah.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church 1882; Ephesus's Marian doctrine is
  dogma; his phrase "one incarnate nature of God the Word" (a forgery of
  Apollinaris he took for Athanasius) fed later Monophysitism; Chalcedon
  451 read him with Leo. Benedict XVI (Oct 2007): "guardian of exactness".
- Particularity: ruthless in method, a saint in doctrine; the Church
  venerates the teaching, not the temper.
- Feast 27 Jun.

## Basil the Great (c. 330–379)
- Caesarea in Cappadocia; family of saints (Macrina, Gregory of Nyssa,
  Peter of Sebaste); studied Athens with Gregory Nazianzen; baptised
  c. 357, monastic tour, ascetic life at Annesi; priest 362, bishop 370.
- Monastic Rules (Long and Short) still the rule of Eastern monasticism;
  Basileiad, a city of hospitals and hospices; against Arians and the
  emperor Valens ("Perhaps you have never met a bishop"); De Spiritu Sancto
  (the Spirit's divinity, homotimia, without saying "God"); Hexaemeron
  homilies; Liturgy of St Basil.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church; one of the three Holy Hierarchs.
  Benedict XVI (Jul/Aug 2007): the social sermons ("the bread you hold back
  belongs to the hungry").
- Particularity: his silence on the Spirit's "God" was a pastoral economy
  Athanasius defended and Gregory Nazianzen criticised.
- Feast 2 Jan (with Gregory Nazianzen).

## Gregory Nazianzen (c. 330–390)
- Arianzus/Nazianzus; Athens with Basil; ordained by his father against his
  will 361; bishop of Sasima 372 (never took possession); Constantinople
  379: the Five Theological Orations in the Anastasis chapel; presided at
  the Council 381, resigned in its course; retired to Arianzus, poetry.
- Trinity: one ousia, three hypostases; the Spirit is God; Christ
  "what is not assumed is not healed" (against Apollinaris).
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church; "the Theologian" (only John and
  Simeon share the title in the East). Benedict XVI (Aug 2007).
- Particularity: the poet among the Fathers (17 000 verses, De vita sua);
  sensitive, resigned twice; friendship with Basil strained by Sasima.
- Feast 2 Jan.

## Gregory of Nyssa (c. 335–after 394)
- Basil's younger brother; rhetor, married (Theosebia?), bishop of Nyssa
  372 by Basil's will; deposed by Arians 376, restored 378; prominent at
  Constantinople 381; funeral orations at court.
- Works: Life of Moses (epektasis: endless progress into God), On the
  Making of Man, Great Catechesis, Against Eunomius, On the Soul and the
  Resurrection, Homilies on the Song of Songs and the Beatitudes; first to
  argue slavery is against the image of God.
- Church's view: saint, not Doctor; Constantinople II 553 named him "father
  of fathers". Leanings to apokatastasis (from Origen) have been rejected by
  the Church without a condemnation of him (Photius, Germanus of
  Constantinople pleaded interpolation). Benedict XVI (Aug/Sep 2007).
- Particularity: the mystical Father; darkness of Sinai as the way to God.
- Feast 10 Jan.

## Athanasius (c. 300–373)
- Alexandria; deacon at Nicaea 325; bishop 328 to 373; five exiles, 17 years
  in all (Trier, Rome, the desert monks, Alexandria's tombs); "Athanasius
  contra mundum".
- De incarnatione ("God became man so that man might become god"), Orations
  against the Arians, Life of Antony (the first monastic biography; read by
  Augustine's friends), Festal letter 39 of 367: the first list of the 27
  New Testament books.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church; "Father of Orthodoxy". Benedict XVI
  (Jun 2007). The Athanasian Creed is not his (Latin, 5th c.).
- Feast 2 May.

## Ephrem the Syrian (c. 306–373)
- Nisibis; deacon; catechist under bishop Jacob; moved to Edessa when
  Nisibis passed to Persia 363; died of plague nursing the sick.
- Hymns (madrashe) on Faith, Paradise, the Nativity, against heresies, sung
  by women's choirs; commentaries on Genesis and the Diatessaron;
  theology in images, not concepts: Mary's virginity and sinlessness, the
  Eucharist as fire and Spirit; "Harp of the Holy Spirit".
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church 1920 (the only Syriac one). Benedict
  XVI (Nov 2007): the poet-theologian.
- Feast 9 Jun.

## Tertullian (c. 155–after 220)
- Carthage; lawyer's mind, converted c. 195; married; probably a priest;
  Apologeticus 197 ("the blood of Christians is seed"); De praescriptione
  haereticorum (the rule of faith, Scripture belongs to the Church);
  Adversus Praxean: coined "una substantia, tres personae", "Trinitas";
  first Latin theological vocabulary (sacramentum, satisfactio).
- Rigorism led him to Montanism c. 207 (the New Prophecy: no second
  penance, no remarriage, no flight in persecution); later formed his own
  group, the Tertullianists (Augustine).
- Church's view: not a saint, never Doctor; the CE: the most brilliant
  Latin before Augustine; read for his orthodox works. Benedict XVI (May
  2007): "a great Father", his fall "a warning" to the intellectual who
  cannot bear the Church's slowness.
- Particularity: "What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?"; the soul
  "naturally Christian"; a traducianist on the soul's origin.

## Clement of Alexandria (c. 150–215)
- Athens (probably), converted, travelled, settled in Alexandria under
  Pantaenus, headed the catechetical school c. 190; left in the persecution
  of 202; died in Cappadocia.
- Trilogy Protrepticus, Paedagogus, Stromata (unfinished miscellany);
  philosophy as a preparation for the Gospel ("the Greeks' Old Testament");
  the "true gnostic" who knows by faith and love against the Gnostics'
  secret knowledge; Quis dives salvetur (wealth is neutral, attachment is
  the fault).
- Church's view: never Doctor; his name was in the Roman Martyrology (4
  Dec) until Clement VIII removed it (1584/1600) on Baronius's advice for
  suspect passages (subordinationism, a docetic-sounding Christ who "did not
  need to eat"); Benedict XIV upheld the removal (1748) while calling him
  learned and holy in life. Benedict XVI (Apr 2007) treats him with honour.
- Particularity: the first Christian to call philosophy a divine gift; his
  "gnostic" language was later misread.

## Cyprian of Carthage (c. 200–258)
- Rhetor at Carthage, converted 246 (Ad Donatum), bishop 248/9; hid in the
  Decian persecution 250 (criticised); the lapsi: neither the confessors'
  cheap peace nor Novatian's no peace, but penance under the bishop;
  councils at Carthage.
- De unitate Ecclesiae 251 ("you cannot have God as Father unless you have
  the Church as mother"; ch. 4 exists in two recensions, one stressing Peter
  more, both probably his); De lapsis; letters. Plague of 252: organised
  relief for pagans too.
- Rebaptism dispute 255–256 with Pope Stephen: Cyprian and the African
  councils rebaptised converts from heresy; Stephen forbade it ("nihil
  innovetur"). The Church sided with Stephen (Arles 314; Augustine: Cyprian
  erred but kept charity). Martyred 14 Sep 258 under Valerian, the first
  bishop martyr of Africa.
- Church's view: saint and martyr; named in the Roman Canon. Benedict XVI
  (Jun 2007): the first bishop to teach on the Church from lived unity.
- Feast 16 Sep (with Cornelius).

## Irenaeus of Lyon (c. 135/140–c. 202)
- Smyrna, heard Polycarp (who heard John); priest in Lyon, carried the
  martyrs' letter to Rome 177; bishop of Lyon after Pothinus's martyrdom;
  mediated the Quartodeciman dispute with Pope Victor 190; martyrdom
  under Severus is late tradition.
- Adversus haereses (five books against the Gnostics): apostolic
  succession, the rule of faith, the Church of Rome's "potentior
  principalitas", the four Gospels; Demonstration of the Apostolic
  Preaching. Recapitulation: Christ sums up Adam, Mary unties Eve's knot;
  "the glory of God is a living man"; the goodness of creation and of the
  flesh; chiliast (a literal millennium; the Church did not follow him).
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church, "Doctor unitatis", 21 Jan 2022
  (Francis). Benedict XVI (Mar 2007).
- Feast 28 Jun.

## Justin Martyr (c. 100–165)
- Flavia Neapolis (Shechem), pagan; philosophic search (Stoic, Peripatetic,
  Pythagorean, Platonist), converted through an old man on the seashore;
  taught in Rome in the philosopher's cloak; First and Second Apology,
  Dialogue with Trypho; beheaded with six companions under Junius Rusticus
  c. 165 (Acts of the martyrdom survive).
- Logos spermatikos: the seed of the Word in every man, so Socrates was a
  Christian before Christ; the first description of the Sunday Eucharist
  (1 Apol 65–67); Old Testament theophanies were the Logos (against Trypho).
- Church's view: saint and martyr; Benedict XVI (Mar 2007): the philosopher
  of Christianity as "the true philosophy".
- Feast 1 Jun.

## Ignatius of Antioch (d. c. 107)
- Second (or third) bishop of Antioch; condemned under Trajan, taken to
  Rome to the beasts; on the road seven letters (Ephesians, Magnesians,
  Trallians, Romans, Philadelphians, Smyrnaeans, Polycarp).
- The threefold ministry (bishop, presbyters, deacons); "do nothing without
  the bishop"; the Eucharist "the flesh of our Saviour"; first use of
  "catholic Church" (Smyrn. 8); Rome "presiding in love"; against docetism.
  To the Romans: do not save me, "I am God's wheat".
- Church's view: saint and martyr, Apostolic Father; named in the Roman
  Canon. Benedict XVI (Mar 2007).
- Feast 17 Oct.

## Clement of Rome (fl. c. 96)
- Third successor of Peter (Linus, Cletus, Clement); identified by some
  with Phil 4,3; Letter to the Corinthians c. 96 in the name of the Roman
  Church, over a revolt against the presbyters at Corinth: order,
  succession from the apostles, humility; read in Corinth as Scripture for
  a century. Later legend: exiled to Crimea, drowned with an anchor.
- Church's view: saint, Apostolic Father; the letter is the first exercise
  of Roman authority over another church. Benedict XVI (Mar 2007).
- Feast 23 Nov.

## Hilary of Poitiers (c. 310–367)
- Pagan, converted through Scripture, married, bishop c. 350; refused to
  condemn Athanasius, exiled by Constantius to Phrygia 356–360; learned
  Greek theology there; De Trinitate (12 books), De synodis (conciliatory
  to the semi-Arians, homoiousios read in an orthodox sense), commentaries
  on Matthew and the Psalms; hymns.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church 1851, "Athanasius of the West".
  Benedict XVI (Oct 2007).
- Particularity: read the Old Testament theophanies as the Son; on the
  Passion held that Christ's body did not feel pain as ours does (a point
  the Church did not follow).
- Feast 13 Jan.

## Leo the Great (d. 461)
- Tuscan; deacon under Celestine and Sixtus III; pope 440–461; met Attila
  at Mantua 452 (Rome spared), Genseric 455 (no burning, no massacre).
- Tome to Flavian 449: two natures, one person, each nature acting what is
  proper to it; the Council of Chalcedon 451 acclaimed "Peter has spoken
  through Leo"; rejected canon 28 (Constantinople's rank). Sermons: the
  Christian's dignity ("Christian, recognise your dignity"); primacy of
  Peter's see as Peter's continuing voice.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church 1754. Benedict XVI (Mar 2008).
- Feast 10 Nov.

## John Damascene (c. 675–749)
- Damascus under the caliphate; his family Christian officials, he perhaps
  treasurer to the caliph; monk at Mar Saba near Jerusalem c. 725; priest.
- Three Discourses against the iconoclasts (726–730): matter is not evil,
  "I do not venerate matter but the Creator of matter who became matter";
  veneration (proskynesis) vs worship (latreia); the Fount of Knowledge
  (Dialectic, Heresies incl. Islam as a heresy, Exact Exposition of the
  Orthodox Faith: the first summa, source of Peter Lombard and Aquinas);
  homilies on the Dormition (Mary's assumption); hymns of the Easter canon.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church 1890; Nicaea II 787 vindicated his
  icon doctrine. Benedict XVI (May 2009).
- Feast 4 Dec.

## Thomas Aquinas (1224/5–1274)
- Roccasecca, Aquino; oblate at Monte Cassino; Naples, Dominican 1244 over
  his family's resistance (held a year in a castle); Paris and Cologne under
  Albert the Great; master at Paris 1256, Orvieto, Rome, Paris, Naples; stopped
  writing Dec 1273 ("all I have written seems like straw"); died at Fossanova
  7 Mar 1274 on the way to Lyon II.
- Summa theologiae (512 questions, 2 669 articles, unfinished), Summa contra
  gentiles, commentaries on Aristotle and on Scripture (Job, Psalms, Isaiah,
  Jeremiah, Matthew, John, Paul), the Catena Aurea (continuous gloss of the
  four Gospels out of the Fathers, Greek and Latin, made for Urban IV
  1263–1268), the office of Corpus Christi (Pange lingua, Adoro te).
- Faith and reason as two orders of one truth; grace perfects nature; five
  ways; analogy; the Eucharist as transubstantiation.
- Church's view: Doctor of the Church 1567 (Pius V), Doctor communis,
  Doctor angelicus; Leo XIII "Aeterni Patris" 1879 made him the norm of
  Catholic philosophy; CIC 252 §3 and OT 16 keep him the teacher of
  theology. "Nothing but yourself, Lord."
- Particularity: denied the Immaculate Conception as it was then framed
  (sanctification after animation), later dogma 1854 in Scotus's line; a
  medieval, not a Father, in this reader.
- Feast 28 Jan.

## Theophylact of Ohrid (c. 1050–after 1107)
- Chalcis (Euboea); deacon at Hagia Sophia, tutor to the porphyrogennetos
  Constantine Doukas; archbishop of Ohrid (Bulgaria) c. 1078 to his death,
  complained of the "barbarians" yet defended his flock's rights.
- Commentaries on the whole New Testament (except Revelation), the minor
  prophets; sober, dependent on Chrysostom, plain and useful; Erasmus used
  him (the "Vulgarius" of his notes), Aquinas cites him in the Catena.
- Church's view: saint in the Orthodox Church (31 Dec); not in the Roman
  calendar, but read by Catholics as a witness to the Greek tradition;
  moderate toward the Latins on the Filioque (a difference of words, he
  said, not of faith).

## Cornelius a Lapide (1567–1637)
- Bocholt (Flanders); Jesuit 1592; taught Scripture at Louvain 1596–1616,
  then at the Roman College to his death, 12 Mar 1637.
- Commentaries on all Scripture except Job and the Psalms (published
  1614–1645), drawing on the Fathers, the rabbis, the moral and mystical
  senses side by side; long the preacher's standard; English translation
  of the Gospels by Mossman/Cobb 1876–1908 (the source of the Catena Bible
  extracts).
- Church's view: no cause; respected commentator of the Counter-Reformation
  school, cited by Haydock and in the "Great Commentary".

## George Leo Haydock (1774–1849)
- Cottam (Lancashire), recusant family; Douai (fled the Revolution 1793),
  Crook Hall; ordained 1798; missions at Ugthorpe (Yorkshire), Whitby,
  Westby, Penrith; suspended 1831–1839 over a dispute with his bishop,
  restored; died at Penrith 29 Nov 1849.
- The Haydock Bible 1811–1814 (Manchester, Thomas Haydock): Douay-Rheims
  with a commentary compiled from the Fathers, Calmet, Estius, Menochius,
  Tirinus, Witham, and his own apologetic notes against Protestants and
  deists; the most popular English Catholic Bible of the 19th century,
  reprinted in the US (Lincoln's inauguration Bible legend is spurious; the
  1859 Haydock is a family Bible of the era).
- Church's view: a parish priest and compiler; the notes are pre-critical
  and polemical, read today as a witness of Catholic apologetics.

## John Cassian (c. 360–435)
- Scythia Minor (Dobruja) or Gaul; monk in Bethlehem c. 380, then Egypt
  (Scete) for over a decade; deacon under Chrysostom at Constantinople,
  pleaded his case in Rome 405; priest; founded St Victor at Marseille
  c. 415 (a men's and a women's house).
- Institutes (the coenobite's life, the eight vices) and Conferences (the
  desert elders on prayer, discretion, purity of heart), written for the
  Gallic monks; Benedict prescribes them for reading (RB 42, 73); De
  incarnatione against Nestorius at Leo's request.
- Conference 13 holds that the beginning of faith can come from man
  (against Augustine's late works); Prosper attacked it; the Council of
  Orange 529 condemned semi-Pelagianism without naming him.
- Church's view: saint in Marseille and the East; in the Roman Martyrology
  (23 Jul) under Benedict XVI's 2004 edition; never Doctor. Benedict XVI
  does not devote an audience to him; the CE calls his teaching in
  Conference 13 the error "which bears his name".

## Second batch (7 Sep 2026)

### Cyril of Jerusalem (c. 315–386)
Priest under Maximus, bishop of Jerusalem c. 350; exiled three times (357 by
Acacius of Caesarea, 360, 367–378 under Valens); at Constantinople 381,
which vindicated his orthodoxy. 24 Catecheses (18 Lenten to the
candidates, 5 mystagogical to the newly baptised on baptism, chrism,
Eucharist, liturgy), the fullest picture of fourth-century initiation.
Avoided "homoousios" (semi-Arian party at first) but taught the Son's
divinity; Eucharist: "what seems bread is not bread but Christ's body".
Doctor of the Church 1883 (Leo XIII). Benedict XVI (Jun 2007): integral
catechesis of doctrine, life and sacrament. Feast 18 Mar.

### Hippolytus of Rome (c. 170–235)
Roman presbyter, perhaps Irenaeus's pupil, Origen heard him c. 212. Logos
theology against the modalists (Noetus, Sabellius), accused Popes
Zephyrinus and Callistus of modalism and laxity; from 217 head of a rival
party (the first "antipope"). Exiled with Pope Pontian to Sardinia 235,
reconciled, both died there, brought home together 13 Aug, venerated as
martyrs. Works: Refutation of all heresies (Philosophumena), commentary on
Daniel (the earliest surviving Christian commentary), on the Antichrist,
the Apostolic Tradition (attribution disputed); the statue in the Vatican
Library is a restored figure of doubtful identity. Feast 13 Aug (with
Pontian). Saint and martyr, his schism forgiven by his end.

### Eusebius of Caesarea (c. 260–339)
Pupil of the martyr Pamphilus in Origen's library; bishop c. 313; sat at
Constantine's right at Nicaea 325, gave the opening address; subscribed
the creed with reservations; defended Arius before and worked against
Athanasius after (Tyre 335). Ecclesiastical History (10 books, the sources
of the first three centuries), Chronicle, Praeparatio and Demonstratio
evangelica, Onomasticon, Life of Constantine, commentaries on Isaiah and
the Psalms, Gospel canons. Not a saint (Arian sympathies, court theology).
Benedict XVI (Jun 2007): "first historian of Christianity", "greatest
philologist of the ancient Church"; history as Christocentric.

### Peter Chrysologus (c. 380–450)
Imola; bishop of Ravenna c. 433 (named by Sixtus III), the imperial court
of Galla Placidia; "golden-worded". 176 short homilies on the Gospels,
the Creed, the Our Father; daily communion; Mary's virginity; Rome's
primacy (his letter to Eutyches 449: "Peter lives and presides in his
see"). Doctor of the Church 1729 (Benedict XIII). Feast 30 Jul.

### Rabanus Maurus (c. 780–856)
Mainz; oblate at Fulda, pupil of Alcuin at Tours (who named him Maurus
after Benedict's disciple); abbot of Fulda 822–842, archbishop of Mainz
847–856; "praeceptor Germaniae". Commentaries on most of the Bible
compiled from the Fathers, De universo (encyclopaedia), De institutione
clericorum, martyrology, poems (Veni Creator Spiritus is ascribed to him),
De laudibus sanctae crucis (figure poems). Synod of Mainz 848 condemned
Gottschalk's double predestination. Blessed in local calendars (4 Feb),
never formally canonised. Benedict XVI (Jun 2009): faith touches the whole
person, the beauty of the liturgy.

### Alcuin of York (c. 735–804)
York cathedral school under Ælberht, master there; met Charlemagne at
Parma 781, head of the palace school at Aachen, the mind of the
Carolingian renaissance; abbot of St Martin at Tours 796; died 19 May 804.
Textbooks on grammar, rhetoric, dialectic; revised the Vulgate text and
the Roman sacramentary (the Gregorian supplement); commentaries on Genesis,
the Psalms, the Song, John; letters, poems. Fought Adoptionism (Felix of
Urgel recanted at Aachen 799). Told Charlemagne faith cannot be forced on
the Saxons. "Blessed" in some calendars, no formal cult; feast 20 May
(Anglican). Not a saint in the Roman calendar.

### Remigius of Auxerre (c. 841–908)
Monk of Saint-Germain at Auxerre, pupil of Heiric (the Carolingian school
line Haimo → Heiric → Remigius); taught at Auxerre, Reims (893, called by
Fulk), Paris. Commentaries on Genesis, the Psalms, the minor prophets,
Matthew; glosses on Boethius, Martianus Capella, Donatus; adapted
Eriugena. Grammar and the liberal arts as the way into Scripture. No cult.

### Haimo of Auxerre (d. c. 865)
Monk of Saint-Germain, Auxerre; commentaries on Paul, the Apocalypse,
Isaiah, the minor prophets, the Song; long printed under the name of Haymo
of Halberstadt (Migne PL 116–118); a source of the Glossa and of Adso's
letter on the Antichrist; real presence in the Eucharist; the Catena Bible
lists him as "Haymo". Never canonised.

### Lactantius (c. 250–325)
African rhetor, called by Diocletian to Nicomedia, lost his chair in the
persecution of 303; tutor of Constantine's son Crispus at Trier c. 317.
Divinae institutiones (the first Latin summa of Christian teaching,
addressed to the educated pagan), De opificio Dei, De ira Dei, De
mortibus persecutorum (the deaths of the persecutors; Constantine's
vision). "Cicero christianus" (the humanists). Weak in doctrine (Jerome:
"would that he had taught our things as well as he destroyed the
others'"): chiliasm, a dualist reading of the devil, silence on the Holy
Spirit; the Gelasian Decree lists his works among the apocrypha. Not a
saint.

### Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109)
Aosta; monk at Bec under Lanfranc 1060, prior 1063, abbot 1078;
archbishop of Canterbury 1093; exiled twice over investiture (William
Rufus, Henry I), settled 1107. Monologion, Proslogion ("that than which
nothing greater can be thought", the ontological argument, rejected by
Aquinas and Kant, kept by Bonaventure, Descartes, Hegel), Cur Deus homo
(satisfaction: the God-man alone can repay what sin owes), De conceptu
virginali (original sin; his pupil Eadmer took the Immaculate Conception
further), prayers and meditations. "Fides quaerens intellectum", "credo
ut intelligam". Doctor of the Church 1720 (Clement XI), "Doctor
magnificus". Benedict XVI (Sep 2009). Feast 21 Apr.

### Bernard of Clairvaux (1090–1153)
Fontaines near Dijon; entered Cîteaux 1112 with thirty companions;
founded Clairvaux 1115, 68 daughter houses in his lifetime; counsellor of
popes (Innocent II's schism), preached the Second Crusade at Vézelay 1146
(its failure: De consideratione to Eugene III); had Abelard condemned at
Sens 1140; died 20 Aug 1153. 86 Sermons on the Song of Songs (to ch. 3),
De diligendo Deo, De gratia et libero arbitrio, Marian homilies (Missus
est: "look at the star, call on Mary"; the "aqueduct"). Christ-centred
mysticism: "Jesus is honey in the mouth". Canonised 1174, Doctor of the
Church 1830 (Pius VIII), "Doctor mellifluus". Benedict XVI (Oct 2009).
Particularity: opposed the new feast of the Conception of Mary (letter
to the canons of Lyon 1140) because he held sanctification after
conception, as Aquinas would; the dogma of 1854 went the other way.
Feast 20 Aug.

### Glossa Ordinaria (12th century)
The standard marginal and interlinear gloss on the whole Latin Bible,
compiled at Laon (Anselm of Laon, † 1117, and his brother Ralph) and
finished at Paris in the second half of the 12th century (Gilbert the
Universal on the Old Testament, Peter Lombard on the Psalms and Paul).
Long wrongly attributed to Walafrid Strabo (Beryl Smalley). Sources:
Augustine, Jerome, Gregory, Bede, Rabanus, Haimo, Origen in Latin.
Aquinas's "Gloss" citations; first printed Strasbourg 1480/81 (Rusch).
Not a person: the reader shows it with initials, no bio, an explanation
page instead.

### Richard Challoner (1691–1781)
Lewes, Sussex, a Presbyterian father, converted with his mother; Douai
1705–1730 (student, professor, vice-president); London mission 1730 in
disguise among the poor; coadjutor 1741, vicar apostolic of the London
District 1758–1781; hid from the Gordon rioters 1780; died 12 Jan 1781,
reburied in Westminster Cathedral 1946. Garden of the Soul 1740 (the
English Catholic prayer book for two centuries), Memoirs of Missionary
Priests 1741 (the martyrs), Meditations, and the revision of the
Douay-Rheims 1749–1752 that modernised its Latinate English (the text most
"Douay" Bibles print, incl. this reader's DRB) with short notes. Cause:
declared Venerable? (no; proposed, not advanced). The reader's DRB notes
are his.

### Erasmus of Rotterdam (1466/9–1536)
Illegitimate son of a priest; Augustinian canon at Steyn 1487, priest 1492,
left the cloister with dispensation; Paris, England (More, Colet), Italy,
Louvain, Basel, Freiburg. Greek New Testament 1516 (Novum Instrumentum,
the first printed Greek NT, with a new Latin and Annotations; the
Paraphrases 1517–1524 read in every English parish under Edward VI),
Enchiridion militis christiani 1503, Praise of Folly 1511, Adages,
editions of Jerome, Cyprian, Ambrose, Augustine. Refused Luther's break
(De libero arbitrio 1524 vs. Luther's De servo arbitrio); "I laid the egg
Luther hatched" (attributed). Paul IV's Index 1559 banned all his works,
Trent's Index 1564 only some (Colloquia, Praise of Folly); scholars today
read him as a Catholic reformer who stayed. Not a saint. His Annotations
are the source of the "Erasmus" entries in the Catena Bible.

### Ambrosiaster (fl. 366–384)
The anonymous Roman commentator on the thirteen Pauline letters (and
Quaestiones Veteris et Novi Testamenti), under Pope Damasus; passed under
Ambrose's name until Erasmus doubted it (1527); the identity is unknown
(Hilary the deacon? Isaac the Jew? Evagrius of Antioch?). Literal,
historical, sober; on Rom 5,12 "in quo omnes peccaverunt" read as "in
whom (Adam) all sinned", the source of Augustine's formula of original
sin; used by Pelagius too. No cult, an author's name only.

### Pseudo-Chrysostom / Pseudo-Augustine / Pseudo-Jerome / "a Greek expositor"
The Catena Aurea's labels for material Aquinas cited under a name modern
scholarship denies him: the Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum (an Arian
Latin author c. 400–450 under Chrysostom's name), sermons in the Augustine
appendices, the Breviarium in Psalmos under Jerome, and unnamed Greek
scholia. No bio pages; the reader explains the label.
